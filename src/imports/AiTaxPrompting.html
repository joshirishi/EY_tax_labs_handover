Prompt like a Pro - Elements

Each element is a lever — pull the right ones for the right task.

Click any tab below each element to reveal details — they stay open once clicked.

1
Persona
WHO should AI be?

Defines who the AI should act like — setting its expertise, seniority, and perspective. A tax partner writes differently from a junior analyst.

Aligns output to the expertise level you need. Without it, AI defaults to a generic voice that doesn't match your audience.

"Explain impact of New Tax Act on MNCs."

"You are a senior tax partner in India. Explain impact of withholding tax changes in the New Income Tax Act, 2025 on MNCs."

2
Context
WHAT's the background?

Background information for the task — the who, what, where, and when surrounding your query.

Without context, AI gives generic answers that miss your specific situation entirely.

"Explain recent changes to transfer pricing regulations."

"Our client in India provides IT support to its parent in Singapore. Explain recent TP Regulation changes in 2025."

3
Instruction
WHAT should AI do?

A clear task or command — the specific action you want AI to perform. No ambiguity.

Define what "significant" or "recent" means — don't leave it to AI to guess.

"Summarise significant recent tax exposures of the Indian target company"

"Summarise tax exposures above INR 25 crore, under dispute in the last 3 assessment years."

4
Constraints & Boundaries
WHAT are the limits?

Setting limits on scope, detail, or length — guardrails that keep AI focused.

Without limits, AI may produce 2000 words when you needed 200.

"Summarise GST refund changes."

"In under 200 words, summarise July 2025 GST refund changes for exporters."

5
Grounding / Source Anchoring
WHERE should AI look?

Instructing AI to use specific statutes, circulars, or case law as its reference base.

Prevents hallucination and ensures legal accuracy. Ungrounded output is dangerous output.

"Explain safe harbour rules."

"According to the Income-tax Act, 1961 and latest CBDT circulars, explain safe harbour applicability to cross-border service fees."

6
Tone / Style
HOW should it sound?

Directing AI to adopt a formal, client-ready, or simplified style matching your audience.

A CFO needs different language than an internal audit team or ITAT bench.

"Draft an email to the client regarding new GST slab rates"

"Explain new GST slab changes in formal and concise manner, suitable for Tax Head of a Logistics company"

7
Output Format
WHAT shape should the answer take?

Specifies desired format — table, bullets, email, memo, comparison chart, etc.

Output is immediately usable without reformatting — saves editing time.

"Compare old vs new tax rates."

"Provide a table comparing old vs new tax rates, followed by 3 bullet-point risks and recommendations."

8
Iterative Refinement
REFINE — don't restart

Improving output through follow-ups — treating AI conversations as iterative, not one-shot.

First drafts are starting points. Each refinement sharpens precision and usability.

"Summarize attached case law"

Step 1: "Summarize facts, issues and ruling." → Step 2: "Now create 5-bullet summary for Indian MNCs."

Same AI. Two Very Different Results.

The only thing that changed? The way you asked.

Prompt like a Pro — Techniques

Now that you know the elements, here are 8 techniques to level up your prompting game.

Click any tab to reveal details.

1
Few-Shot Prompting
SHOW AI what good looks like

Providing 1-3 examples of ideal input-output pairs before asking your actual question — so AI learns the pattern you want.

Like showing a new associate a sample memo before asking them to draft one — the output matches your style and standard.

"Draft a tax equalisation policy for employees relocating from India to the US."

"Here is a sample tax equalisation policy [attached]. Using the same format and structure, draft a policy for India-to-US relocations."

2
Iteration
BUILD on what AI gives you

Using multi-turn conversations — asking AI to improve, expand, or restructure its own previous output step by step.

First drafts are starting points. Each follow-up sharpens precision — like reviewing a junior's memo through rounds of feedback.

"Summarize this SC ruling and draft a client memo."

Turn 1: "Summarize the facts and ruling." → Turn 2: "Now draft a 1-page client memo." → Turn 3: "Simplify for a non-tax CFO audience."

3
Chain of Thought
MAKE AI show its reasoning

Asking AI to "think step by step" — making it show its reasoning before giving the final answer so you can verify the logic.

Like asking an associate to show their workings, not just the conclusion. You can spot errors in reasoning before they reach the client.

"What is the effective tax rate for MNCs with royalty payments?"

"Think step by step: First identify applicable provisions, then calculate base rate, add surcharge and cess, factor in DTAA, and arrive at the effective rate for MNCs with royalty payments."

4
Meta Prompt
ASK AI to write the prompt for you

A prompt that instructs AI to generate an optimised prompt for you — describe your use case and let AI craft the perfect structured instruction.

You don't need to remember every technique. Describe what you need and let AI build the optimal prompt structure for you.

"Help me respond to a GST show cause notice on product classification."

"You are an expert prompt crafter. My use case: I'm a Tax Head responding to a GST SCN on misclassification. Craft me an optimal prompt that includes persona, context, task, constraints, and step-back reasoning."

5
Refinement
SHARPEN the output after review

Asking AI to critique and improve its own output — reviewing as a partner would, fixing gaps, strengthening arguments.

AI catches its own weak spots. Like asking an associate to self-review before submitting — the second pass is always stronger.

"Draft a TP memo for IT services." (accept whatever comes back)

"Now review your own draft as if you are the reviewing partner. Identify gaps in legal reasoning, strengthen weak arguments, and produce an improved version."

6
Creative Expansion
EXPLORE angles you haven't considered

Asking AI to brainstorm alternative approaches, counterarguments, or edge cases you may not have considered.

Uncovers blind spots in your analysis. Like having a second opinion from a specialist in a different tax domain.

"Analyze the tax implications of this cross-border restructuring."

"Analyze the restructuring, then list 5 risks I might be overlooking — including GAAR, PE exposure, and indirect transfer provisions that a revenue officer might raise."

7
Audience Targeting
TAILOR for who will read it

Explicitly telling AI who the reader is — so it adjusts complexity, jargon level, and depth accordingly.

A board presentation needs different language than an ITAT submission. Name your reader and AI writes for them.

"Explain POEM rules under Section 6(3)."

"Explain POEM rules under Section 6(3) in simple language for a CFO with no tax background. Avoid section references — focus on business impact and what action they need to take."

8
Flipped Prompting
LET AI ask YOU the questions

Instead of you writing the prompt, ask AI to interview you — it asks clarifying questions first, then produces a tailored output.

When you don't know how to frame the problem, let AI guide you. It surfaces context you might have forgotten to include.

"Help me with a tax restructuring advisory." (dumps everything at once, misses key details)

"I need help with a restructuring advisory. Before you start, ask me 5 clarifying questions about the entities, jurisdictions, transaction type, timeline, and key concerns."

The Golden Rules

Prompt Engineering — Do's & Don'ts

A practical guide for tax professionals. Hover each card for a real example.

✅ Do's
📌 Be Specific & Give Context
Mention your industry, jurisdiction (e.g. India — GST / IT Act), entity type and financial year. The more context you give, the more relevant and accurate the output.
"I am the Tax Head of an Indian pharma company (₹2,000 Cr turnover). Explain Section 43B(h) implications for MSME vendor payments in FY 2025-26."
🏗️ Structure Your Ask
Use a clear format: "I am a [CFO / MD]. My situation is [X]. Please explain [Y] in simple terms." A structured prompt consistently yields a structured, usable response.
"I am a CFO. My company received a transfer pricing adjustment of ₹45 Cr. Explain my appeal options at CIT(A) and ITAT in 5 bullet points."
📊 Define the Output Format
Specify what you need — a comparison table, a bullet checklist, or an executive summary. AI will match the format you request, saving you editing time.
"Provide a table comparing old vs new GST rates for logistics services, with columns: Service, Old Rate, New Rate, Effective Date, Impact."
🔄 Iterate and Refine
Treat it as a dialogue. Follow up with "Simplify this", "Focus only on MSME applicability" or "Add a practical example." Each refinement improves precision.
"That's good, but simplify the language for a board presentation. Remove all section references and focus on business impact only."
🔒 Anonymise Sensitive Data
Replace actual PAN, GSTIN, client names and financials with dummy figures before prompting. You still get meaningful insights without compromising confidentiality.
"Company A (manufacturing, turnover ₹X Cr) has paid ₹Y Cr to Company B (US parent) as management fees. Analyze withholding obligations."
🤝 Validate With Your Advisor
Use AI for first-level research and drafting only. All final decisions on filings, compliance positions and strategy must be confirmed with your qualified tax advisor.
"Use this AI output as a starting point for discussion with your EY tax team — never as standalone advice for filings or board decisions."
❌ Don'ts
🚫 Don't Be Vague
"Tell me about taxes" produces generic output. Instead: "Explain the impact of Section 43B(h) on MSME vendor payments for FY 2024-25 for a private limited company."
❌ "What are the latest tax changes?" → AI doesn't know which country, which tax, which year, or which entity type you mean.
🚫 Don't Stack Multiple Questions
Asking ten questions in one prompt produces ten diluted answers. Break it down — one focused question at a time delivers sharper, actionable output.
❌ "Explain GST, income tax changes, TDS rates, advance tax dates and MAT provisions for 2025-26" — this will get shallow answers on everything.
🚫 Don't Enter Confidential Data
Real PAN, GSTIN, client names, account numbers and actual financial data must never be entered into a public AI tool. Data confidentiality is non-negotiable.
❌ Never type: "PAN: AABCX1234Z, GSTIN: 27AABCX1234Z1ZP, actual assessment order details" into ChatGPT or Gemini.
🚫 Don't Treat AI as Final Authority
AI can generate plausible but incorrect information, including outdated tax provisions. Never use AI output directly for returns, filings, or board-level decisions.
❌ AI may confidently cite a section number that was amended or a circular that was superseded. Always cross-verify with current law.
🚫 Don't Omit Jurisdiction
Tax rules vary significantly by country, state and entity type. Without a clear jurisdiction, AI defaults to generic — and often inapplicable — answers.
❌ "What's the capital gains tax rate?" — For whom? India resident? NRI? Company? On what asset? Which holding period? Specify everything.
🚫 Don't Accept the First Response as Final
Always probe the output. Ask "Which section covers this?" or "Is this current for AY 2025-26?" Critical review of AI responses is part of responsible usage.
❌ Copy-pasting AI's first draft into a client email without reviewing for accuracy, tone, and completeness is a professional risk.

⚠️ FOR INFORMATIONAL PURPOSES ONLY. ALWAYS CONSULT YOUR QUALIFIED TAX ADVISOR BEFORE ACTING ON ANY AI-GENERATED OUTPUT.

Recap in a Nutshell

Putting your #BestPrompt Forward

Your 8-element checklist. Before you hit Send, make sure you've covered these.

👤 Persona

Tell AI WHO to be. Like telling a new colleague: "Pretend you're a senior tax partner" — so it talks like one, not like a Wikipedia article.

📋 Context

Give the background story. Like telling a taxi driver WHERE you're going — without it, AI drives in circles giving generic answers.

📝 Instruction

Say exactly WHAT to do. Like ordering food: "Give me a paneer tikka" works. "Give me something nice" doesn't.

🚧 Constraints

Set boundaries. Like telling a kid "draw me a picture — but only use 3 colours and keep it on one page." Keeps AI focused.

⚖️ Grounding

Tell AI WHERE to look. Like saying "only use THIS textbook for answers" — prevents it from making things up.

🎨 Tone / Style

Tell AI HOW to sound. Like asking someone: "Explain it like I'm presenting to a CFO" vs "Explain it to a 5-year-old." Same info, different packaging.

📊 Output Format

Tell AI WHAT SHAPE the answer should take. Like saying "give me a table, not a paragraph" — saves you 20 minutes of reformatting.

🔄 Iterative Refinement

Don't restart — refine. Like editing a draft: "Make it shorter", "Add a table", "Simplify for the board." Each follow-up makes it better.
